## (b) Key details

### The fund
Situational Awareness LP (SEC CIK 0002045724, San Francisco) is the AI-focused hedge fund founded in 2024 by Leopold Aschenbrenner, a former OpenAI superalignment researcher and author of the viral 2024 essay *Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead*, which forecast AGI by ~2027 and superintelligence shortly after. The fund is the essay's thesis expressed as capital: long the physical layer of AI (compute, power, memory), at times heavily leveraged. [secondary reporting]

### Performance (all after fees, as stated in the letter unless noted)
| Period | Return | Source |
|---|---|---|
| H1 2026 (Jan–Jun) | **+439%** net | [secondary reporting — FT review of the Jul 24 letter] |
| July 2026 alone | **≈ –67%** (unaudited interim estimate in the July letter) | [secondary reporting] |
| Q3 2026 | **–65%** | [WSJ via reporting] |
| YTD through Sep 30, 2026 | **+88.5%** | [WSJ via reporting] |
| Q4 / October 2026 | Not in the letter (period ends Sep 30). The book's 9 remaining underlyings fell roughly –20% to –58% during October's AI rout | [secondary reporting — CNBC, Nov 16] |

Arithmetic check: +439% then –65% ≈ +88.5% YTD — internally consistent. Essentially the entire Q3 loss occurred in ~3 weeks of July; Aug–Sep combined were roughly +6%. [secondary reporting — ZeroHedge]

### What happened in July 2026
- The fund ran roughly **4x leverage** [secondary reporting] on a book concentrated in AI infrastructure; at June 30, SanDisk (28.0%) and Micron (27.5%) alone were >55% of its disclosed US holdings. [13F filing — Q2 values; percentages via secondary reporting of the same filing]
- A sharp July AI selloff (SanDisk fell ~47% in July) triggered margin pressure from its lenders — reported as Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America. [secondary reporting — CNBC]
- The fund **sold the bulk of its public equity book to Citadel** (Ken Griffin) in block trades, reportedly below market prices; reported assets fell from ~$45B at the start of July to ~$10B. Citadel later unwound most of the acquired risk via ~100 block trades worth >$4B (Reuters, Aug 21). [secondary reporting]
- The fund came, in the July letter's words, "closer to permanent capital impairment than is acceptable to us"; it removed all leverage, closed its shorts, and kept its private positions — above all its **Anthropic** stake, the one position no margin clerk could force it to sell. [secondary reporting]
- Days before the blowup, a July 24 letter had invited clients to add money on Aug 1, flagging "a particularly good time to add funds." [secondary reporting]

### Risk changes announced in the Q3 letter
- Portfolio restructured "for resilience"; the firm spent recent months **not accepting new investor money**, "focused inward to learn lessons and build a better chassis." [WSJ via reporting]
- **Hiring an experienced chief risk officer** (the firm reportedly had none while running a ~$45B levered book; headcount is now ~30, up from 8 in June). [WSJ via reporting; headcount secondary]
- New stated principle, verbatim: **"Volatility, yes; existential risk, even if we are totally wrong, never."** [WSJ via reporting]
- The fund **expects eventually to resume shorting** (a major source of July's pain) and **possibly resume borrowing, at lower levels** than before. [WSJ via reporting]
- Note the tension: the FT has separately reported "Situational Awareness 2.0" planning a **return to leveraged trading** with fresh outside capital in the new year; JPMorgan reportedly cut the fund off in September, and it has held prime-brokerage talks with Jefferies and Clear Street. [secondary reporting — FT]

### The predictions (all from the Q3 letter, as reported)
1. **Frontier labs exit 2027 with a revenue run-rate above $1 trillion.** Current market valuations, he argues, imply only modest lab growth — a "radical disconnect"; October's selloff made the implied expectations lower still. [WSJ via reporting]
2. **Most companies will lose "most of their value"** as superintelligence and recursive self-improvement (AIs building more advanced AIs) make employees and intellectual property obsolete as sources of advantage. [WSJ via reporting]
3. **Named as exposed:** Nvidia (called out by name in the WSJ account), plus AMD, Microsoft, Apple, and the "Elonverse" — Tesla and SpaceX. [WSJ via reporting; full six-name list via Benzinga/Newsis summaries]
4. **"A civilization of alien geniuses" arrives by 2028, barring a regulatory ban on AI.** [WSJ via reporting — the letter's closing prediction]
5. **"For most of the rest of the world, 2027 might be the last year that feels vaguely normal."** (verbatim) Things are "starting to feel very weird" in San Francisco. [WSJ via reporting]
6. Near-term picture: hundreds or thousands of AI agents running long-term projects, overseen by other agents; AI models beginning to contribute to advances in compute scaling. [WSJ via reporting]
7. Aside that became the letter's most-quoted line: **"Perhaps the best 'buy and hold' asset we can come up with is the Mona Lisa."** [WSJ via reporting]
8. Current opportunities **"are the highest potential return we have seen in the lifetime of the fund."** (verbatim) [WSJ via reporting]
9. On itself: "Many of our predictions, which seemed like science fiction two years ago, came true. And yet we also learned how wrong we could be, and how merciless the market is when we are." (verbatim) [WSJ via reporting]

### Portfolio and the Q3 13F (verified from the filing itself)
- **Mix after Anthropic's IPO: ~70% public stocks / 30% private companies** — the inverse of August, when the fund was mostly private. Note the mechanism: much of the "flip" is Anthropic itself being reclassified from private to public when it listed (IPO priced $83 on Nov 18; opened $111.50). [WSJ via reporting; IPO prices secondary]
- **Anthropic is the largest position and the top conviction:** the fund "is highly bullish at the IPO price — and even from a blank slate would make it our largest position by some margin"; it may trim/rebalance it like any stock. [WSJ via reporting — verbatim quote]

## (d) Questions for opinion

1. **Thesis vs. vehicle:** The letter's core claim is that superintelligence/RSI dissolves incumbent moats and moves value to the frontier-model layer. Is that directionally right — and if so, on what horizon does it become *investable* rather than merely true-eventually?
2. **The $1T run-rate call:** Frontier labs exiting 2027 above a $1T annualized revenue run-rate — what does that require in growth rates from today's base, and is the market really pricing "only modest growth," or pricing execution/regulatory risk the letter dismisses?
3. **Internal contradiction?:** The letter says infrastructure moats (Nvidia et al.) will be destroyed by the very force its own book is long (neocloud, power, memory via calls). Is the rebuilt portfolio a coherent "own the bottlenecks until the bottleneck era ends" trade, or a thesis at war with itself?
4. **Risk reform credibility:** Fully-paid calls genuinely remove margin-call risk — but the same concentrated names, bought weeks after the blowup, then fell 20–58% in October. Is "volatility yes, existential risk never" actually satisfied by this book, and does the reported leveraged "2.0" relaunch void the letter's central promise?
5. **Anthropic concentration:** The fund's rescue came from the one illiquid position it could not be forced to sell. Is making that same name the largest position "by some margin" from a blank slate conviction — or survivorship bias dressed as strategy? What is the single-name/private-market risk now that it has IPO'd?
6. **Timeline discipline:** AGI-by-2027 / "alien geniuses" by 2028 / 2027 as the last normal year — which of these dated claims is most falsifiable soonest, and what observable evidence in 2026–27 would confirm or kill the letter's worldview before its portfolio has to survive another path?
