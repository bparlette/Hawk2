# FRAMING RULING — minimal pusher & patient (Cherie, Nov 23, 2026)

Cherie's words: "I'm ok with seeing as little of the pusher and patient
as possible to get the point across they are in the bed or chair or
whatever."

## What this changes
- The earlier "keep the current framing" ruling (Nov 23, 07:55) is
  relaxed: Cole and Maya do NOT need to stay large in frame.
- Minimal character presence is acceptable — even preferred — as long as
  the situation reads instantly: he is in the bed / in the wheelchair /
  in the ambulance, she is the one pushing.
- Camera may pull back or reframe toward the environment where that
  helps play. A wider view is a legitimate tool for the fairness fix
  (shooters enter the visible zone sooner) — see
  OPUS-FIX-FAIRNESS-PACE.md.

## What still binds
- The situation must never be ambiguous: if a first-time viewer at phone
  size can't tell he's in a bed/chair within a second, it's too little.
- Patient-eyes effects (neck, lids, blur) are unchanged — this ruling is
  about how much of the characters is in frame, not about the camera's
  body feel.
- Neon grades against this ruling: small or partly-out-of-frame
  pusher/patient is NOT a defect when the bed/chair/ambulance reads.
