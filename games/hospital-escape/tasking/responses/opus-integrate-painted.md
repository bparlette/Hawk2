# Opus response: the painted cast in the live game (OPUS-INTEGRATE-PAINTED.md + the REALISM BAR amendment)

**Base build:** the audited fix build `1fc2441` plus the consciousness amendment (`opus-consciousness-amendment.md`, same push).

**Proofs** are in `tasking/responses/opus-integrate-painted-shots/`. Every capture is in-engine, 390×844, `?seed=7` unless noted, taken at a paused frame.

## What ships
- **`game/painted.js`**: the billboard system.
  - One textured quad per character, turned to the camera around the vertical axis only.
  - Each frame is grounded on its **own measured bottom row**.
  - Raycasts (player shots and fairness line of sight) hit the **painted silhouette only**: a per-frame alpha mask throws away hits on the transparent part of the quad.
  - It also carries the realism-bar layers (§4).
- **`game/painted-data.js`**: generated by **`tools/painted-data.py`** from the PNGs, so it's reproducible.
  - Per frame: the measured bbox and bottom, the feet centre, the MANIFEST muzzle/barrel/flash coordinates, Maya's measured grip points, and an 8 px alpha mask.
  - 25 KB in total.
- **Wiring in `game/main.js`:**
  - `paintCast`: Hale, Maya and Cole per beat;
  - `paintEnemy`: the enemy state machine;
  - `gripSolve`: Maya's hands on the push bar;
  - the enemy-fire hook: flash, light and telegraph from the painted barrel;
  - two establishing shots.
  - `index.html` loads the two new scripts. three.js is still **only** the pinned CDN 0.128.0, and there are no new dependencies.
- **Fallback.** A character keeps its procedural body until **all 20** textures have loaded.
  - If any texture fails, the procedural cast plays and nothing else changes. `?painted=0` forces the fallback.
  - Note: Chromium refuses CORS image loads from `file://`. The painted cast therefore appears over http(s), which includes GitHub Pages, or with `--allow-file-access-from-files`. Our Playwright tools now pass that flag and wait for `window.__painted`.

## 2. The audit notes
1. **Maya's grip: it reads with placement alone, so Neon does not owe a hands-on frame.**
   - **Method (`gripSolve`).** For each push frame I measured the hand centre (`GRIP` in `tools/painted-data.py`: fingertip extreme minus 18 px, rows 320–400). Hand height is 1.04–1.06 m above that frame's own bottom.
   - The hand point is placed **on the camera→push-bar ray**, at the depth where its painted height meets that ray. Her feet are then set from the measured hand-to-feet offset on the billboard plane.
   - So from the camera her painted hands land **on** the bar in every frame of the stride, wherever the camera is.
   - To make that possible I **lowered the bed's push bar from 1.12 m to 1.0 m** (a real bed's push-bar height) and gave it two posts (`art.js`).
   - **Seeing her at all.** The play rig sits 1.7 m behind the rider, so the pusher is out of frame (FRAMING RULING: fine). The **bed push therefore opens on an establishing shot**:
     - the camera is pulled back 1.35 m, 0.4 m left and 0.3 m up, behind Maya at the bar;
     - it holds for 1.9 s, then dollies into the play rig by 3.4 s;
     - it is presentation only: rig, speeds and waves are untouched, and the first target is still at 5.7 s.
   - **Proof:**
     - `bed-push-grip.png`, with `bed-push-grip-zoom2x.png`: hands on the bar;
     - `push-cycle-grip.mp4`: 3.6 s, the stride and the grip through the dolly-in;

2. **Anchored to measured bottoms, never the nominal 720.** Bottoms are re-measured from the PNGs (alpha ≥ 64) and agree with the MANIFEST within 1–2 px:

## 3. Muzzle flashes on the gun
When an enemy shot leaves (`enemyShot`):
- the enemy is switched to its **fire frame immediately**;
- the flash sprite and a **70 ms warm PointLight `0xffa042`, radius 3.5 m** (MANIFEST §5) sit on the MANIFEST **flash** coordinate, transformed through the billboard (plain `(446,211)`, scrubs `(61,196)`, mirrored with the figure);
- the shot's **red telegraph tracer starts at the MANIFEST barrel** coordinate (plain `(356,211)`, scrubs `(162,197)`). This is visual only; damage still arrives at the engine's ETA.

The flash is part of the shooter's own billboard and depth-tested in the scene, so it can't float in front of another character who is nearer the camera. The aim tell (glint) uses the aim frame's muzzle.

`enemy-plain-firing.png` was taken at the plain shooter's red (damaging) shot, the first damage of the bed push. `enemy-scrubs-firing.png` shows two scrubs, one mid-flash and one aiming, with a third down. It was taken in the chair corridor, seed 7, with the autopilot off so they could close in. The scrubs shot captured there was visual-only. **`enemy-plain-sequence.mp4`** follows `bed_w0_0` from spawn to down: peek 1.2 s → aim 1.37 → flinch 2.03 → aim 2.27 → **fire 2.83** → flinch 2.97 → peek/aim cover cycles → flinch 9.2 → **down 9.5**. That is the whole peek→aim→fire→flinch→down chain in one take.

## 4. REALISM BAR: assets, not stickers
1. **Contact shadow:** under every painted character, anchored at the measured baseline, centred on the frame's bbox and sized from its width. It is a soft radial gradient drawn on the floor.
2. **Wet-floor reflection:** a quad lying **on** the floor in front of each near figure (within 16 m, fading out from 9 m). Its shader traces the camera's mirrored ray into the billboard's own plane, so it shows the figure's true mirror image for this camera:
   - clipped at the baseline;
   - fading with height above it (0.55 m e-fold);
   - mip-blurred (bias 2.2);
   - darkened to 62% of the sprite tint;
   - **depth-tested like the floor it lies on**, so a counter in front hides it.

   The ambulance bay floor is steel, so the braced Cole has none. You can see it under Hale and Maya in `hale-opening-*.png`, and under the plain shooter's boots.
3. **Lighting match:**
   - every sprite takes the scene's grade: indoor ×(0.90, 0.95, 1.00), outdoor ×(0.76, 0.81, 0.92);
   - it takes **the scene fog**, so distant figures dim and haze like the walls;
   - it **warms in real time** with the muzzle lights near it (`PT.lightAt`): the shooter's own 70 ms flash light and the player's muzzle flash.

   It then goes through the same post chain as the world: DOF from the depth buffer, bloom, grade, ACES, lids and grain.
   **What it can't do:** the corridors have no real local lights (hemisphere plus emissive panels), so there is no per-position "stands under a lamp" term. The grade plus fog makes no sprite brighter or warmer than its room in any capture I took.
4. **Feet and skating:**
   - **Maya's push:** stride phase is locked to distance travelled, the frame's own bottom is on the floor, and the grip solve moves her with the bed.
   - **Not solvable with this pack:** enemies that relocate (cover advance of about 1–2 m, rushers) **glide on a static frame**, because the pack has **no enemy walk or run frames**. **That is a one-item Neon fix: a 4-frame walk cycle each for `enemy-plain` and `enemy-scrubs`, same cell, scale and baseline conventions.** Once it lands, wiring it is the same stride-by-distance code Maya uses.
5. **Depth, not screen space:** sprites are real meshes that depth-test and depth-write. Pillars, beds, counters, cover props and door frames cover them when the world says so. They also block line of sight through their painted pixels only (the mask), not the transparent quad.
6. **Edges:** blended alpha edges (cut at α < 0.06) instead of a hard cut-out, so there is no fringe. Mipmaps, plus the post DOF and breathing softness, give distant figures the scene's haze and blur.

**Verdict, judged at 390×844:**
- The bed push, both fire captures, the opening and the transfer read as people in the room.
- **The honest weak spot is `ambulance-braced-establishing.png`.** The braced pose is painted from above with the legs toward the viewer, so on a flat billboard he reads as *seated on the bay floor* only from a high camera. I raised the shot to match (the camera looks down into the bay). He now has his hand and heels on the floor, but it is still the most "cut-out" of the set.
- **Named Neon fix (optional):** a braced frame painted from a standing eye-line behind the rear doors, or a matching floor-contact shadow baked into the frame.
