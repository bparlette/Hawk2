# Hospital Escape — PATIENT-EYES camera spec (Cherie, Nov 23, 2026)

The camera is Cole's eyes. He is a sick, shot-up patient with a weak neck.
Framing/size of the heroes does NOT change (Cherie's ruling: keep current framing).
This spec is about FEEL — what his eyes and neck do.

## The neck
- Hunched and weak: the view sits low, tilted slightly down, never level-and-steady.
- Constant small wobble and drift; the head lags behind movement and overshoots
  slightly on turns, then settles — like he can't hold his head up properly.
- Getting shot = a head jolt, then a slow, effortful re-centre.

## The vision
- Focus breathes: blur drifts in and out on a slow, uneven cycle (already partly
  in pass 2 — measure it, don't assume).
- The more he is shot, the worse it gets: more blur, double vision, longer
  out-of-focus stretches.
- Near death the picture dims and colour drains toward a narrow, dark tunnel.

## The eyelids (the signature effect — NEW, not in pass 2)
- Black occlusion creeps in from the top and bottom edges of the screen like
  heavy eyelids. Coverage grows as health drops.
- Blinks: brief closures that get longer and more frequent the more hurt he is.
  Each hit taken forces a hard blink / flinch of the lids.
- When he is shot a lot, the black turns and blinks RED — blood in the eyes.
  Sustained fire = the lids droop toward closed, pulsing red-black.
- Floor rule: during active play the lids never fully close except for blink
  instants — the player must always be able to play. Full closure is reserved
  for the lose state: the eyelids shut → "COLE IS DOWN".

## The ambulance (beats 7–9, worst in the back, beat 8)
- Same patient, now riding: the head sways WITH the vehicle — lateral roll and
  lag into every turn, woozy drift on straights, hard to concentrate and hard
  to hold aim centred.
- Sway amplitude grows with speed, turn sharpness, and damage taken.
- Eyelids and blur stack on top of the sway — this is the worst he feels
  in the whole game.

## The route (Cherie, Nov 23, added same morning)
The game must never feel like moving forward in a straight line.
- Inside the hospital: constant turns — left and right through hallways,
  open areas (cafeteria, waiting room, lobby) crossed at angles, doorways
  taken on the diagonal, bends and double-backs. The bed and the chair
  are pushed around corners, not down a tube.
- On the road: the escape turns — corners, S-curves, intersections. The
  ambulance leans into them and the patient's head sways with them
  (see the ambulance section above).
- Grade it concretely: per beat, how many real turns, and what is the
  longest straight stretch? Name the beats that feel like a corridor
  on rails.
