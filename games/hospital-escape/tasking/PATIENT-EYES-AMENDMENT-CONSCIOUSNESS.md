# AMENDMENT to PATIENT-EYES-SPEC — it's consciousness, not blinking
(Cherie, Nov 23, 2026)

Cherie, on the eyelid ruling: "I think it's less about blinking and
more about going in and out of consciousness."

## The reframe
The lid system is TWO separate layers, and they must not be conflated:
1. **Ordinary blinks** — small, brief, involuntary. Unchanged.
2. **Consciousness fades** — the big closures. Cole is a gunshot patient
   drifting in and out. A fade is not a blink: it comes on heavy and
   slow, holds, and the return is a swim — light and focus coming back
   unevenly, the neck slack while he's out and weakly re-centring as
   he returns (tie neck-spring strength to consciousness level).

## Tuning direction
- Fades are driven by hurt: deeper and longer as HP falls, spiking
  after heavy hits — not one identical snap per hit.
- At full fade he is OUT: brief, black, muffled. Then he swims back.
- The lose state stays as specced: the fade that doesn't come back,
  then COLE IS DOWN.

## Fairness consequence (closes the audit's carve-out)
The fairness gate (OPUS-FIX-FAIRNESS-PACE) excluded blink instants from
the visible zone. Under this reframe that carve-out is closed for
fades: **no damaging shot may land while Cole is fully out** (lids at
or near full closure). Enemies may fire — sound and tracers keep the
pressure — but damage applies only once he is at least partly back
(lids below ~85% closed). Hits landing on the way down or the swim
back, while he can partly see, are fair.
(This paragraph is Moth's default reading of the reframe — if Cherie
flips it, this file gets corrected.)

## Supersedes
This amendment overrides the PATIENT-EYES-SPEC floor rule ("lids never
fully close in play except blink instants; full closure = lose only").
Full closure CAN now happen in play — as a fade-out, briefly, per
above. Everything else in the spec stands.

## Proof owed in the response
Captures or short clips at three hurt levels showing: fade depth and
duration scaling with hurt, the swim-back, and the neck slackening
while out. Response goes in the same fix response file or a short
follow-up response — builder's choice, named in the fix response.
